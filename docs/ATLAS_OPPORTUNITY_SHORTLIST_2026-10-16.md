# Atlas Zero-Capital Opportunity Shortlist

Research snapshot: 2026-10-16

## Evidence standard

These are hypotheses, not validated customer demand. Public reports show broad problems and adoption patterns; they do not prove that a particular buyer will pay for any one product. No customer outreach, account creation, publication, spend, or payment processing has been performed.

## Public evidence reviewed

1. Federal Reserve Banks, *2026 Report on Employer Firms: Findings from the 2025 Small Business Credit Survey* (published 2026): nearly half of firms reported using AI; among AI users, accuracy and adapting tools to business needs were prominent challenges, and implementation time was a concern for firms planning adoption.
   - https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/survey/2026-report-on-employer-firms
2. U.S. Chamber of Commerce, *2026 Empowering Small Business: The Impact of Technology on U.S. Small Business* (published October 2, 2026): reported broad technology-platform use and growing AI adoption among small businesses. This is a survey report and does not establish demand for any specific proposed product.
   - https://www.uschamber.com/technology/2026-empowering-small-business-report
3. Federal Trade Commission, *Cybersecurity for Small Business*: free guidance covers data protection, passwords, MFA, backups, updates, and incident planning.
   - https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/small-businesses/cybersecurity
4. National Institute of Standards and Technology, *Cybersecurity Basics*: foundational small-business practices include MFA, strong passwords, backups, updates, and phishing/ransomware preparation.
   - https://www.nist.gov/itl/smallbusinesscyber/cybersecurity-basics
5. GitHub ReadME Project, *Overcoming human error with code automation and testing*: describes repeated developer lifecycle tasks from linting through deployment.
   - https://github.com/readme/guides/code-automation-and-testing

## Selected first experiment

**Small-business AI workflow starter kit.** It is the best first test because it can be created as static, local-only templates with no API keys, customer data, hosted service, or spend. It addresses the gap between AI interest and implementation identified by the Federal Reserve survey. The evidence is indirect: the hypothesis is that a concise workflow planning kit is useful, not that customers will pay for it.

### Falsification criteria

- The sample is too generic to help someone complete a concrete task.
- A user cannot understand how to adapt the checklist without additional paid tools or specialist help.
- The package makes claims about accuracy, compliance, security, or savings that it cannot demonstrate.
- No clear buyer or repeated use case can be identified from additional permitted public research.

### Guardrails and next steps

1. Create a private sample kit and test it against a fictional workflow only.
2. Review for clarity, completeness, and unsupported claims.
3. Record time-to-complete and defects; do not report those as customer outcomes.
4. Do not publish, message prospective customers, create accounts, collect personal data, or spend money without separate, specific approval.
5. Revenue remains $0 until actual receipts are verified.
